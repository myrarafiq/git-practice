# Software Development & EdTech Revolution Article

## Article

[How EdTech Revolution Is Reshaping the Education Industry](https://radixweb.com/blog/edtech-revolution-software-development-is-reshaping-education-industry)

## Why I Found This Article Interesting

I chose this article because EdTech is an area I am genuinely interested in, and I liked how it looked at software development as something that can actually change the learning experience rather than just move existing education online.

One line that stood out to me was: “Technology is not driving change; it is accelerating it.” I thought this was a pretty good way of describing what is happening in education. The article goes beyond just talking about technology making education more convenient and looks at how things like AI, data analytics, personalized learning, real-time feedback, and digital classrooms can change how students actually learn. I especially found the discussion around personalized learning interesting because software can use information about a student's progress, strengths, and weaknesses to create a learning experience that is more suited to them instead of treating every student the same.

The section on AI and data analytics also stood out to me. The article describes how these technologies can help identify learning patterns and provide insights that would be difficult to get from traditional methods alone. I thought the connection between this and real-time feedback was particularly interesting. Instead of a student completing something and only finding out later that they misunderstood it, technology can potentially identify the problem as they are learning and respond immediately. The article also discusses accessibility, which I think is an important part of the conversation because technology can make educational resources available to students who may not have had the same access through traditional systems.

I've had some exposure to this space myself through co-founding an EdTech startup and interning at EdTech companies, so I've gotten to see a little bit of how these ideas work from the product and data side. For example, at Lemma Education, we were working on an AI math tutor that could listen to how a student was solving a problem and give feedback on their reasoning rather than only checking the final answer. Seeing that alongside the article's discussion of personalized learning and real-time feedback made the ideas feel much more practical to me.
